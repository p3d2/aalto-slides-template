## Type

<!-- layout: theme=black -->

<!-- slice: page_section_element -->

Typeface and the way is it used plays a key role in Aalto University’s visual identity system. Inter is open-source typefaces and is freely available for use.

<!-- layout: alignment=left, theme=neutral -->

<!-- slice: page_section_element -->

###### Typefaces

<!-- layout: alignment=left, theme=neutral -->

<!-- slice: page_section_element -->

###### Inter

Inter is Aalto University’s typeface intended for all communication needs regardless of media. Inter is an open-source sans serif typeface family that is highly legible as well as versatile in its selection of weights. Inter also features over 2000 glyphs and covers 147 languages.
To keep things simple Bold and Regular are selected as the primary typefaces whereas other weights should be used only as an exception where purposeful.

###### Substitute for Inter / Arial

Arial is intended as a backup typeface family replacing Inter in situations where the use of primary typefaces is impossible or limited, such as Office programs, document letterheads, e-mails, etc. Arial is a system typeface and can be found as default on all contemporary operating systems.

###### Typography guidelines

<!-- layout: alignment=left, theme=neutral -->

<!-- slice: page_section_element -->

Typography is at its best when its form serves the message you wish to deliver. A reader should be able to quickly distinguish between headings and body text, which requires a sufficient size hierarchy.
Always make titles larger than the copy. However, try to keep the number of different type sizes at a minimum to avoid an overly complicated look. A recommended starting point is to limit the use of different type sizes to a maximum of three on any visual surface.

- Make titles larger than the body text. 
- Keep paragraphs aligned left, ragged right. 
- Do not use overly many font sizes on a single document.
- Don’t use UPPER CASE letters
- Don’t capitalize every word of a title
- When a person's name is mentioned for the first time in the copy text, the name should be bolded.

<!-- layout: alignment=left, theme=neutral -->

**2) Short intro texts and quotes** / Inter Regular
**3) Subheadlines** / Inter Bold, tracking -25
**4) Bodycopy** / Inter regular

Paragraph: Align left, ragged right

###### Text that needs special emphasis

Expressive typography should be reserved exclusively for compact visual tidbits. It should never be used for long titles, headlines, or lengthy paragraphs.

Key facts and figures should be emphasized with bold and large type.

###### Use of typefaces in general

Aalto University’s typography should be clear, consistent, and hassle-free, supporting any specific content in an approachable, intentional, and systematic manner. The overall style is purposely unadorned to let the content speak for itself.

On a general note, it is highly recommended to refrain from using too many type sizes or styles as well as word- or letter-spacings.

Keep it simple, align left, and stick to as few styles and sizes as needed.

###### Complementary typeface / Besley

Besley is an alternative typeface that is intended to be used in publications when there is a need for serif fonts. Besley is also an open-source slab-serif typeface family.

To keep things simple Bold and Regular are selected as the primary typefaces whereas other weights should be used only as an exception where purposeful.

###### Use of colour

<!-- layout: alignment=left, theme=neutral -->

<!-- slice: page_section_element -->

Black typography should always be used on either white or backgrounds using the brand colours, and white typography on black backgrounds.
This ensures that there is enough contrast between the colour and background and makes the use of colours accessible both for digital and print.
